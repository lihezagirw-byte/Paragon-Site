Talent can become a job. We want young Rwandans to know that.

We are five students at African Leadership University. Through our E-Lab course, we are studying why so many educated young people in Rwanda can’t find work, and building something to help.

Who we are

Team Paragon is a group of five ALU students who share one question: why does education not always lead to work?

Our mission

By 2029, we want to bring the unemployment rate of educated people in Rwanda down from 35% to 20%. We want to do it by helping young people depend less on job hunting.

We encourage students to build their talents, because talent can turn into income, jobs and investment, in areas like tourism and cultural preservation.

Our vision

A Rwanda where every young person sees their talent as a real opportunity, and where graduates create jobs instead of waiting for them.

The problem we are working on

In Rwanda, going to school does not guarantee a job. The numbers below come from the National Institute of Statistics (NISR) and from youth data for 2025.

35%of university graduates are unemployed (NISR, 2021)
30%of high school graduates are unemployed (NISR, 2021)
24.5%of young people were not in work, education or training in 2025

Graduates are out of work more often than people with no formal education, at 22%. That tells us education alone is not producing economic growth. Many graduates wait for a job that matches their degree, or simply don’t know what opportunities exist, so a lot of talent goes unused.

We believe that investing in arts, design and culture, and in the young people who work in them, could lower unemployment a lot by 2029.

What we found, and what we want to build

We interviewed five young people in Kigali in person and asked about their experience starting out.

What young people told us

They described a fear of failing, little money to start with, not enough information, and trouble finding customers.

Why the gap stays open

Opportunities exist, but they are weakly connected to the training, mentorship and funding that would help young people start their own work.

Our solution

A digital platform that brings these things together in one place:

  • jobs and opportunities
  • financial literacy
  • mentorship
  • access to markets

We would share it through schools, social media and community meetings, so it reaches young people where they already are.
